## About

My work sits between machine learning research and practical system building. I
am most interested in problems where structured outputs, visual understanding,
and language interact: recognizing handwritten mathematics, aligning text with
molecular representations, and building training pipelines that make
experiments easier to reproduce, inspect, and extend.

I value controlled ablations, clear evaluation, and honest descriptions of what
a model has—and has not—demonstrated. Beyond model development, I enjoy turning
research workflows into reliable code, from data preparation and experiment
tracking to efficient fine-tuning and evaluation.

## Selected Work

### [Mask CoMER: Enhancing Handwritten Mathematical Expression Recognition with Masked Language Pretraining and Regularization](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-04624-6_22)

**Co-first author · ICDAR 2025 · LNCS 16025.** A two-stage approach combining
masked-language-model pretraining with stochastic-depth regularization. It
reached ExpRates of 64.56%, 63.03%, and 65.22% on CROHME 2014, 2016, and 2019,
respectively—up to 5% above the CoMER baseline.

### [ChemAligner-T5: A Unified Text-to-Molecule Model via Representation Alignment](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-21625-0_16)

**Co-first author · ICCIES 2026 · CCIS 2943.** A BioT5+-based text-to-molecule
model that uses contrastive learning to align textual and molecular
representations. On the L+M-24 test set, it achieved 69.77% BLEU and a 31.28%
Levenshtein distance.

### [HMER-Baselines](https://github.com/Purin1410/HMER-Baselines)

A public initiative to organize HMER baselines around cleaner training and
evaluation workflows. Its current README defines the reproducibility goal and
the intended direction for optimized pipelines and Kaggle-ready experiments;
implementation is still being developed.

## Current Direction

My demonstrated work is currently centered on HMER and cross-modal learning. My
next research direction is **language-grounded industrial visual inspection**:
using vision-language models to find and explain manufacturing defects from
natural-language descriptions, with an emphasis on zero- and few-shot
generalization. This is a direction I am actively developing toward—not a claim
of deployment or validation on real factory data.
