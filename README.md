## About

My work sits between machine learning research and practical system building. I am
most interested in problems where structured outputs, visual understanding, and
language interact: recognizing handwritten mathematics, aligning text with molecular
representations, and building training pipelines that make experiments easier to
reproduce, inspect, and extend.

I value controlled ablations, clear evaluation, and honest descriptions of what a model
has—and has not—demonstrated.

## Publications

### LexiChem

**First author · APWeb-WAIM 2026 · accepted.** A text-to-molecule model evaluated on the
L+M-24 benchmark, developed from my undergraduate capstone project.

### CorrTie: Correction-aware Tie-breaking for Active Learning with Vision-Language Models

**Co-author · APWeb-WAIM 2026 · accepted.** An acquisition strategy for active learning
with vision-language models. It improves accuracy by 7.14, 3.74, and 0.66 percentage
points at 1%, 2%, and 5% labelling budgets.

### [ChemAligner-T5: A Unified Text-to-Molecule Model via Representation Alignment](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-21625-0_16)

**Co-first author · ICCIES 2026 · CCIS 2943.** A BioT5+-based text-to-molecule model that
uses contrastive learning to align textual and molecular representations. On the L+M-24
test set it reached 69.77% BLEU and a 31.28% Levenshtein distance.

### [Mask CoMER: Enhancing Handwritten Mathematical Expression Recognition with Masked Language Pretraining and Regularization](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-04624-6_22)

**Co-first author · ICDAR 2025 · LNCS 16025.** A two-stage approach combining
masked-language-model pretraining with stochastic-depth regularization. It reached
ExpRates of 64.56%, 63.03%, and 65.22% on CROHME 2014, 2016, and 2019 respectively—up to
5 points above the CoMER baseline.

## Current Direction

My demonstrated work is centered on HMER and cross-modal learning. The direction I am
now developing toward is **language-grounded industrial visual inspection**: using
vision-language models to find and explain manufacturing defects from natural-language
descriptions, with an emphasis on zero- and few-shot generalization.

This is a research direction in progress—not a claim of deployment or validation on real
factory data.
